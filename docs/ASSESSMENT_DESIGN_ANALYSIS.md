# Assessment design analysis (model-based)

> Generated by `tools/design-analysis.js` (seeded, N = 20000 simulated respondents per scenario). **Simulation under assumptions, not evidence about real people.** Real-data analysis uses `tools/psychometrics.js` and the plan in `VALIDATION_PLAN.md`.

## 1. Score resolution (exact, not simulated)

- Each facet has 2 items on a 1-5 scale, so the raw facet sum takes 9 values and the **facet score can only be 0, 13, 25, 38, 50, 63, 75, 88, 100**. A score such as 51 or 98 cannot occur at facet level.
- One response-option step on one item moves a facet score by 12 or 13 points. Facet-level differences smaller than about 25 points (two steps) are within one-item noise by construction.
- Dimension scores average 4 facets and so have finer granularity (steps of about 3), but they inherit the facet noise. A dimension has 8 items in total.
- Skipped items are scored as neutral (3) and therefore pull a facet toward 50.
- All 20 facets pair one positively and one negatively keyed item. This cancels a constant agree-bias within a facet, but also means the two items of a facet are *intended opposites*, so facet reliability cannot be shown with Cronbach alpha (undefined for 2 items); report the inter-item correlation of direction-aligned answers and Spearman-Brown instead.

## 2. Archetype behaviour under purely random answering

Respondents choose each answer uniformly at random (no signal at all).

- Distinct archetypes produced: 36 of 36 defined.
- Most frequent: TWIN_PR 5.8%; TWIN_PI 5.5%; TWIN_PS 5.4%.
- Family shares: Twin 50.4%, Pure 11.5%, Blend 36.6%, Balanced (PRISM) 1.5%.
- Mean displayed confidence: 25.5%; flagged low-signal: 0.0%.
- Reading: random answers still yield a named archetype for nearly everyone, so the archetype label alone is not evidence of a meaningful pattern. The confidence figure and straight-lining flag are the only built-in guard.

## 3. Measurement-noise ceiling (simulated two sittings of the same simulated person)

Each simulated person has fixed true facet levels; each sitting adds independent response noise (sd shown, in answer steps). Facet levels are drawn with the stated between-facet correlation. This estimates how much **measurement noise alone** would limit test-retest stability. Real stability may be lower (mood, context) and the true noise level is unknown until measured.

| Scenario | noise sd | loading | facet r (mean) | dimension r (mean) | same archetype on retest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low noise | 0.6 | 1 | 0.81 | 0.83 | 26% |
| Moderate noise | 0.9 | 1 | 0.68 | 0.70 | 17% |
| High noise | 1.2 | 1 | 0.54 | 0.57 | 11% |
| Moderate noise, weaker trait signal | 0.9 | 0.6 | 0.43 | 0.47 | 11% |

Interpretation guide: published personality inventories typically aim for retest r of roughly .70-.90 on their scales (a convention, check against the chosen reference instruments). If real retest data fall well below the simulated moderate-noise rows, item or format changes are needed before facet-level language is justified.

## 4. What this means for product claims

- Facet scores must be presented as coarse (nine possible values) and dimension scores as the more stable summary.
- Archetype labels can change between sittings even for a stable person; they must be framed as a reflection starting point, not a type.
- Reliability, structure and validity of the real instrument remain **unmeasured**. Claims stay at "structured self-reflection framework" until `VALIDATION_PLAN.md` studies report.
