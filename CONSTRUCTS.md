# Construct definitions (Phase 3)

Status: **definitions for review.** These state what each score is *intended* to describe and what it must not be used to claim. Correspondence to established constructs is a **hypothesis** used to choose reference instruments for validation (see `VALIDATION_PLAN.md`); it is not demonstrated for PRISM. Scores are self-reported answers to two statements per facet, so every definition below reads as 'how the person described themselves in this questionnaire'.

Scoring: facet score = two items, 0-100 (nine possible values, see `ASSESSMENT_DESIGN_ANALYSIS.md`); dimension score = mean of its four facets. Item wording and review: `ITEM_REVIEW.md`.

Model note: PRISM's five dimensions are **not** the Big Five. Some facets resemble Big Five or HEXACO facets, others (Attention, Intuition, Tempo, Distinctiveness) have no clean counterpart. The app must not say PRISM 'is the same architecture as' those models.

## P · Perception

**Intended meaning:** How a person tends to take in and organise information: ideas versus facts, new versus familiar, wide versus narrow, implied versus stated.

**Does not mean:** Cognitive ability, mental health, or how well someone understands situations.

| Facet | High pole ↔ low pole | Intended meaning | Does not mean | Nearest established construct (hypothesis) | Items |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P.abstraction | Conceptual ↔ Concrete | Preference for ideas, models and possibilities over concrete, directly observable facts when thinking and learning. | Intelligence, education level or ability to handle detail. Low scores are not 'less intellectual'. | Openness: Ideas/Intellect (e.g. IPIP Intellect, BFI-2 Intellectual Curiosity) | 1-2 |
| P.curiosity | Explorer ↔ Settled | Attraction to new and unfamiliar experiences versus satisfaction with the familiar. | Willingness to take risks (see R.risk) or persistence with a topic (see M.persistence). | Openness: Adventurousness / curiosity; novelty seeking | 3-4 |
| P.attention | Panoramic ↔ Focused | Self-described breadth of attention: taking in the whole situation versus narrowing to one thing. | Attention-deficit or any attentional disorder; concentration ability; this is a style description, not a capacity. | No clear Big Five counterpart; nearest are attentional-breadth and absorption notions | 5-6 |
| P.intuition | Pattern-reader ↔ Literalist | Tendency to sense patterns and implied meaning ahead of explicit explanation, versus taking information as stated. | Accuracy of intuition; communication skill; any neurodevelopmental trait. 'Literalist' is a style label, not a deficit. | Popular sensing/intuition notion; modest empirical link to Openness, so treat the mapping as weak | 7-8 |

## R · Response

**Intended meaning:** How a person tends to react and move: speed, visibility of feelings, flexibility, and risk appetite.

**Does not mean:** Emotional stability or stress coping.

| Facet | High pole ↔ low pole | Intended meaning | Does not mean | Nearest established construct (hypothesis) | Items |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R.tempo | Quick ↔ Measured | Speed of deciding: moving quickly and adjusting versus pausing and weighing first. | Quality of decisions, intelligence or anxiety. Quick is not better than measured. | Decisiveness / deliberation (low Conscientiousness: Deliberation), impulsivity scales | 9-10 |
| R.expression | Expressive ↔ Reserved | How visible inner feelings are to others versus kept private. | How much a person feels. Reserved people can feel intensely. | Emotional expressiveness; part of Extraversion in some models | 11-12 |
| R.adaptability | Fluid ↔ Steady | Comfort improvising when plans change versus preferring to hold the plan. | Resilience, coping skill or stress tolerance. | Flexibility; low Conscientiousness: Orderliness; Openness to change | 13-14 |
| R.risk | Bold ↔ Cautious | Appetite for bold bets versus protecting against downside before acting. | Recklessness, courage or financial behaviour in any specific domain. | General risk-taking (e.g. DOSPERT-type scales); excitement seeking | 15-16 |

## I · Identity

**Intended meaning:** How a person describes their own stance: independence, firmness of belief, wish to stand out, and sense of continuity.

**Does not mean:** Self-esteem, identity development, or any clinical identity disturbance.

| Facet | High pole ↔ low pole | Intended meaning | Does not mean | Nearest established construct (hypothesis) | Items |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I.autonomy | Independent ↔ Yielding | Reliance on own judgment versus deferring to others. | Social skill, leadership ability or defiance. | Autonomy (self-determination theory); low Dependence (HEXACO) | 17-18 |
| I.conviction | Resolute ↔ Open | How firmly beliefs are held and how readily they change. | Open-mindedness as a virtue, or correctness of beliefs. High scores are not 'stubborn' and low scores are not 'weak'. | Cognitive closure / dogmatism (inverse); intellectual humility | 19-20 |
| I.distinct | Singular ↔ Belonging | Wanting to stand out versus wanting to fit in. | Self-esteem, narcissism or social anxiety. | Need for uniqueness versus need to belong | 21-22 |
| I.stability | Grounded ↔ Fluid | Feeling the same self across situations versus feeling like a different person in different settings. | Emotional stability or mental health. Context-dependence can be flexibility. | Self-concept clarity; cross-role consistency (low self-monitoring) | 23-24 |

## S · Social

**Intended meaning:** How a person says they relate to others: warmth, appetite for company, emotional attunement, and conflict style.

**Does not mean:** Social skill, relationship quality, or compatibility with any other person.

| Facet | High pole ↔ low pole | Intended meaning | Does not mean | Nearest established construct (hypothesis) | Items |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S.warmth | Warm ↔ Detached | Leading with care and openness toward people met, versus keeping cool distance. | Kindness in action, or being cold. Detached people can be reliably caring. | Extraversion/Agreeableness: Warmth (e.g. NEO Warmth) | 25-26 |
| S.gregarious | Sociable ↔ Solitary | Gaining energy from company versus from solitude. | Social skill, shyness or social anxiety. | Extraversion: Gregariousness | 27-28 |
| S.empathy | Attuned ↔ Objective | Feeling others' emotions readily versus approaching situations analytically. | Understanding others accurately, caring behaviour or compassion; analytical people can be highly empathic in action. | Affective empathy (empathic concern, emotional contagion); HEXACO Sentimentality | 29-30 |
| S.harmony | Accommodating ↔ Frank | Smoothing friction to keep peace versus saying hard things directly. | Agreeableness as a virtue, honesty, or conflict skill. | Agreeableness: Compliance / conflict avoidance versus Straightforwardness | 31-32 |

## M · Motivation

**Intended meaning:** What a person says moves them: striving, structure, scale of ambition, and follow-through.

**Does not mean:** Achievement, work performance, or suitability for any role.

| Facet | High pole ↔ low pole | Intended meaning | Does not mean | Nearest established construct (hypothesis) | Items |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M.drive | Striving ↔ Content | Continual reaching for the next goal versus contentment with enough. | Happiness, success or work ethic. | Achievement striving; wanting versus contentment | 33-34 |
| M.discipline | Structured ↔ Spontaneous | Running life on systems and plans versus staying open and spontaneous. | Competence or reliability; spontaneous people can deliver reliably. | Conscientiousness: Orderliness / Self-discipline | 35-36 |
| M.ambition | Ambitious ↔ Modest | Aiming for large, ambitious outcomes versus modest, sufficient ones. | Talent, status or greed. | Ambition / aspiration (partly HEXACO Social Boldness, partly Achievement) | 37-38 |
| M.persistence | Relentless ↔ Mercurial | Finishing what is started versus following new interests and moving on. | Intelligence, grit as a validated construct, or reliability. | Perseverance of effort; Conscientiousness: Industriousness; Grit (Perseverance subscale) | 39-40 |

## Known boundary problems (to test with data)

- **R.adaptability vs M.discipline**: 'stick to the plan' and 'run my life on systems and plans' are nearly the same behaviour.
- **M.drive vs M.ambition**: 'content with enough' and 'aim for enough' are nearly the same statement.
- **S.warmth vs S.gregarious vs S.empathy**: three facets that many models fold into one or two factors.
- **P.attention** and **S.empathy** pair statements that are not logical opposites (see item review), so the facet may measure a mixture.

Each of these is a falsifiable concern: `tools/psychometrics.js` flags facet pairs correlating at or above .70 and facets whose two direction-aligned items correlate below .20.
